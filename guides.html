Everything you need to know about tiers, tests and points.
How to get tier tested

Tier tests run on 130.78.218.210:25606. Type /tiertest, choose a gamemode and your region, and you join that gamemode's queue. A tester in your region takes you to a private arena with the official kit for that mode.

Tests are usually one to three fights. The tester is looking at movement, aim, healing timing and how you handle pressure — not just who wins. A loss can still earn a tier, and a lucky win will not carry you to HT1.

Your tier is added to your profile after the test. You can be tested in every gamemode, and each one is ranked separately. If you stop competing in a mode, the tier becomes RETIRED — it leaves the gamemode list but still counts for overall points.

What HT1 to LT5 mean

Every gamemode has five tiers, and Tier 1 is the best. Each tier is split into High and Low, which gives ten steps:

HT1LT1 HT2LT2 HT3LT3 HT4LT4 HT5LT5

HT1 is the highest rank a player can hold: the small group who define a gamemode. LT1 is still elite but a step below. HT2 and LT2 are strong, consistent players who beat most of the ladder. HT3/LT3 is the middle of the list. HT4/LT4 means you know the kit but still have clear gaps. HT5/LT5 is the entry point — tested and ranked, with room to climb.

Tiers are not permanent. Testers re-evaluate players when they improve, and a player can hold different tiers in different gamemodes.

Points and titles

Every tier is worth points. Add up your best tier in each gamemode and you get your overall score:

TierPointsTierPoints
HT160LT145
HT230LT220
HT310LT36
HT44LT43
HT52LT51

Titles are milestones on the same score: Rookie below 10 points, Combat Novice at 10, Combat Cadet at 20, Combat Specialist at 50, Combat Ace at 100, Combat Master at 250 and Combat Grandmaster at 400.

Reaching 400 points usually means several HT1s or a very broad spread of high tiers, which is why Grandmaster is rare.
